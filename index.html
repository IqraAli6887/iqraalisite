Hi There 👋

I am a PhD researcher in the School of Electronic Engineering and Computer Science at Queen Mary University of London, working in Natural Language Processing and the Centre for Human-Centred Computing under the supervision of Prof. Maria Liakata .

My research focuses on longitudinal modelling, large language models, computational mental health, multilingual NLP, and low-resource language technologies. I am particularly interested in how language models can represent and reason about changes in people, states, and behaviour over time.

I currently lead the CLPsych 2026 Shared Task on capturing and characterizing mental-health changes through social-media timeline dynamics. I also contribute to the Data Science for Mental Health (DS4MH) research community.

Before starting my PhD, I completed a Master's degree at the Nara Institute of Science and Technology (NAIST) in Japan, where I worked in the Natural Language Processing Lab. My work has included dataset and benchmark construction across Pashto, Urdu, Japanese, Swahili, and English, as well as evaluation of multilingual and vision-language models.

I also have research and applied-AI experience at Fujitsu AI Lab, Fusic Co., Ltd., and CureMD Healthcare IT R&D.

Projects

Selected research projects, datasets, and benchmark-development work.

LiFT — Longitudinal Instruction Fine-Tuning

LiFT investigates whether instruction fine-tuning can improve the ability of large language models to perform in-context learning for longitudinal modelling. The work studies model behaviour across several longitudinal datasets and examines how fine-tuning, temporal context, and prompting interact when models must reason about change over time.

[Paper]

CLPsych 2026 Shared Task

A shared-task initiative focused on capturing and characterizing mental-health changes through social-media timeline dynamics. My role includes coordinating task design, annotation frameworks, dataset development, evaluation protocols, baseline systems, and shared-task documentation with an international research team.

HLU — Human vs. LLM-Generated Urdu

HLU is a benchmark for detecting human- and LLM-generated Urdu text. It contains 1,014 instances spanning 13 domains and multiple linguistic granularities, with human evaluation and multilingual model benchmarking.

Low-Resource Pashto Paraphrase Detection

The first manually annotated Pashto paraphrase-detection corpus developed in this work contains 5,793 sentence pairs across ten domains. XLM-RoBERTa fine-tuning achieved an F1 score of 96%, establishing a strong benchmark for sentence-level paraphrase detection in Pashto.

[Paper]

VLURes — Multilingual Vision-Language Benchmarking

VLURes evaluates grounding and cross-lingual robustness in vision-language models across multiple languages and tasks, with particular emphasis on multilingual and low-resource settings.

[Paper]
