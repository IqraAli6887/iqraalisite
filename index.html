Hi There 👋

I am a PhD researcher in the School of Electronic Engineering and Computer Science at Queen Mary University of London, working in Natural Language Processing and the Centre for Human-Centred Computing under the supervision of Prof. Maria Liakata.

My research focuses on longitudinal modelling, large language models, computational mental health, multilingual NLP and low-resource language technologies. I am particularly interested in how language models represent, track and reason about changes in people's psychological states, language and behaviour over time.

My work includes instruction fine-tuning, in-context learning, temporally informed modelling, model evaluation, dataset and benchmark development, and multimodal NLP.

I served as Lead Organiser of the CLPsych 2026 Shared Task on capturing and characterizing mental-health changes through social-media timeline dynamics. I also lead activities within the Data Science for Mental Health (DS4MH) Interest Group, supporting interdisciplinary collaboration between researchers working in artificial intelligence, NLP and mental health.

Before my PhD, I completed a Master's in Engineering at the Nara Institute of Science and Technology (NAIST) in Japan, under the supervision of Prof. Taro Watanabe. My earlier research involved multilingual NLP, low-resource language resources, human-versus-LLM-generated text detection and vision-language model evaluation.

I have also worked on applied AI research at Fujitsu AI Lab, Fusic Co., Ltd., and CureMD Healthcare IT R&D.

Projects

Selected research projects, datasets and benchmark-development work.

LiFT — Longitudinal Instruction Fine-Tuning

LiFT investigates whether instruction fine-tuning can improve the ability of large language models to perform in-context learning for longitudinal modelling.

The work studies model behaviour across several longitudinal datasets, exploring how fine-tuning, temporal context and prompting interact when models must reason about changes over time.

[Paper]

CLPsych 2026 Shared Task

Led an international shared-task initiative on capturing and characterizing mental-health changes through social-media timeline dynamics.

Responsibilities included coordinating task design, annotation frameworks, dataset development, evaluation protocols, baseline systems and shared-task documentation.

[Overview Paper]

HLU — Human vs. LLM-Generated Urdu Text

Developed a benchmark for detecting human- and LLM-generated Urdu text, containing 1,014 instances across 13 domains and multiple linguistic granularities, with human evaluation and multilingual model benchmarking.

[Paper]

Low-Resource Pashto Paraphrase Detection

Developed a manually annotated Pashto paraphrase-detection corpus containing 5,793 sentence pairs across ten domains. Fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa models achieved 96% F1 for sentence-level paraphrase detection.

[Paper]

VLURes — Vision-Language Benchmarking

VLURes evaluates long-text grounding and cross-lingual robustness in vision-language models through six tasks spanning four languages, including multilingual and low-resource settings.

[Paper]
